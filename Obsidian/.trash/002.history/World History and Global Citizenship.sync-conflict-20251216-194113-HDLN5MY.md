Beginning in 1948, Global Citizenship emerged from the aftermath of the world wars, with the [[United Nation]] establishing universal declaration that protect human rights and dignity.
Universal Declaration of Human Rights (UDHR)

Being a global citizen means recognizing the ~={blue}interconnectedness=~ of our actions and their impact on the world.

The United Nations' universal declarations, such as the rights of women (CEDAW - 1979), children (1959), and people with disabilities (CRPD-1975), serve as reminders that we have a shared responsibility to protect and uphold human rights.

Being a global citizen goes beyond geographical boundaries and fosters a sense of belonging to a 
larger community with shared values and goals.

### What is the purpose of studying world history?

Studying world history serves multiple purposes. It helps us understand the interconnectedness of the modern world and prepares students for diverse, global workplaces. By studying the past, we can see how events in one region have impacted others and gain the necessary context to comprehend these complex relationships. World history also fosters intercultural competence and an understanding of diverse historical perspectives, which are essential skills in a globalized economy. Furthermore, it cultivates critical thinking, analytical skills, and an appreciation for different cultures and societies. Finally, exploring world history illuminates the historical roots of contemporary global challenges, such as climate change, inequality, and conflict, providing valuable insights for informed decision-making in our complex world.
