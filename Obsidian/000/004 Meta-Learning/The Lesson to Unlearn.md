## 🚀 The Core Thesis

The most damaging habit learned in school is **substituting "learning" with "getting good grades."** This conditions intelligent people to "hack bad tests"—optimizing for artificial metrics imposed by authorities—rather than doing actual good work. This habit is disastrous in the real world, particularly in entrepreneurship, where results (users, revenue) cannot be faked.

---

## 🔑 Key Concepts

### 1. The Conflation of Learning vs. Grades

- **Theory:** Classes teach you $X$, and tests measure your knowledge of $X$.
    
- **Practice:** Tests are imperfect proxies. Students quickly realize that **studying for the test $\neq$ learning the material.**
    
- **The "Game":** Diligent students ignore interesting digressions or deep dives. They focus solely on "sharply-defined chunks of information" likely to appear on the exam (e.g., specific dates, teacher's biases, old exam questions).
    
- **The Result:** Students compete to maximize the difference between the grade received and the actual learning required.
    

### 2. "Hackable" vs. "Unhackable" Tests

- **Hackable Tests (Bad Tests):**
    
    - Imposed by **authorities** (teachers, admissions officers, middle managers).
        
    - Are proxies for something else (e.g., grades as a proxy for intelligence).
        
    - Can be "gamed" (e.g., college admissions coaching, memorizing keywords).
        
    - _Example:_ Getting an A in History by memorizing a list of causes without understanding the context.
        
- **Unhackable Tests (Good Tests):**
    
    - Imposed by **reality** or **market forces**.
        
    - Measure the outcome directly, not a proxy.
        
    - Cannot be tricked; you either win or you don't.
        
    - _Example:_ A football match (score), a scientific experiment (mass), or a startup (revenue/users).
        

### 3. The Startup Trap

- Founders often fail because they treat startups like school. They try to "hack the test" of raising money or getting press.
    
- **The Misconception:** Founders ask, "How do we trick VCs?" or "How do we launch perfectly?"
    
- **The Reality:** The only way to win in startups is to **make a product people want.**
    
- _Insight:_ VCs and press are not the teachers grading you; the market is. The market cannot be charmed or hacked long-term.
    

### 4. Societal Shifts

- **Old Economy:** Dominated by oligopolies. Success meant climbing the ladder, pleasing bosses, and hacking corporate bureaucracy.
    
- **New Economy:** Erosion of the link between work and authority. Success increasingly comes from **direct value creation** (startups, independent creators).
    
- _Trend:_ As "hacking bad tests" becomes less profitable, education must evolve to teach actual mastery.

## 🧠 Personal Reflections & Connections

- **[[Goodhart's Law]]:** "When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure." This is the economic principle behind "hacking tests."
    
- **[[Signal vs Noise]]:** Grades are often noisy signals of competence. In Data Science, this is akin to overfitting a model to training data (the test) rather than generalizing to new data (the real world).
    
- **Action Item:** Audit current goals. Am I optimizing for a metric (a certificate, a title, a grade) or for the underlying reality (skill acquisition, solving a problem)?
