## Linear Models

> [!info] Definition of a Linear Function
> A function $f$ is a **linear function** if its graph forms a straight line. It is written using the slope-intercept form:
> $$y = f(x) = mx + b$$
> Where:
> - $m$ is the **slope** of the line (representing the constant rate of change of $y$ with respect to $x$).
> - $b$ is the **$y$-intercept** (the initial value where $x = 0$).

### Key Feature: Constant Growth Rate
A defining characteristic of linear functions is that they grow or decay at a completely uniform rate. For instance, for the function $f(x) = 3x - 2$:
Whenever $x$ increases by $0.1$, $f(x)$ increases by exactly $0.3$. The slope $m = 3$ remains invariant across any step index.

---

> [!example] Example 1: Linear Modeling of Atmospheric Cooling
> 
> As dry air moves upward, it expands and cools. Suppose the ground temperature is $20^\circ\text{C}$ and the temperature at a height of $1\text{ km}$ is $10^\circ\text{C}$.
> 
> **(a) Express temperature $T$ (in $^\circ\text{C}$) as a function of height $h$ (in kilometers):**
> 1. Set up the linear base model:
>    $$T = mh + b$$
> 2. Find the intercept using the ground state condition ($T = 20$ at $h = 0$):
>    $$20 = m(0) + b \implies b = 20$$
> 3. Find the slope using the second state point ($T = 10$ at $h = 1$):
>    $$10 = m(1) + 20 \implies m = 10 - 20 = -10$$
> 4. **Resulting Formula:**
>    $$T = -10h + 20$$
> 
> **(b) Interpret the slope and sketch the behavior:**
> - The slope $m = -10^\circ\text{C/km}$ represents the constant rate of change of temperature with respect to height. It indicates that the temperature decreases by $10^\circ\text{C}$ for every $1\text{ km}$ of vertical ascent.
> 
> ![[Pasted image 20260625160938.png|center]]
> 
> **(c) What is the temperature at a height of $2.5\text{ km}$?**
> Substitute $h = 2.5$ into the derived explicit function:
> $$T = -10(2.5) + 20 = -25 + 20 = -5^\circ\text{C}$$

## Polynomials

> [!info] General Definition
> A function $P$ is called a **polynomial** if:
> $$P(x) = a_n x^n + a_{n-1} x^{n-1} + \dots + a_2 x^2 + a_1 x + a_0$$
> Where:
> - $n$ is a nonnegative integer denoting the **degree** of the polynomial (provided the leading coefficient $a_n \neq 0$).
> - The numbers $a_0, a_1, a_2, \dots, a_n$ are constants called the **coefficients**.
> - **Domain:** The domain of any polynomial is $\mathbb{R} = (-\infty, \infty)$.

### Classifications by Degree

| Degree ($n$) | Function Type | Algebraic Form                | Geometric Graph                                                                                   |
| :----------- | :------------ | :---------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **1**        | **Linear**    | $P(x) = mx + b$               | Straight line                                                                                     |
| **2**        | **Quadratic** | $P(x) = ax^2 + bx + c$        | Parabola ($a > 0$: opens upward; $a < 0$: opens downward)<br>![[Pasted image 20260625161621.png]] |
| **3**        | **Cubic**     | $P(x) = ax^3 + bx^2 + cx + d$ | Smooth cubic curve ($a \neq 0$)<br>![[Pasted image 20260625161635.png]]                           |

> [!example] Example 4: Quadratic Modeling of a Falling Ball
> A ball is dropped from the upper observation deck of the CN Tower, $450\text{ m}$ above the ground. Its height $h$ is recorded at $1$-second intervals. Find a model to fit the data and predict the time at which the ball hits the ground.
> 
> **Step-by-Step Solution:**
> 1. **Analyze Data & Scatter Plot:**
>    Plotting tabular metrics points to a non-linear parabolic curve, rendering a linear model inappropriate.
> 2. **Formulate the Quadratic Model:**
>    Using regression methods (such as least squares), we obtain the tracking model:
>    $$h = 449.36 + 0.96t - 4.90t^2$$
> 
> ![[Pasted image 20260625161659.png|center]]
> 
> 3. **Calculate Ground Impact Time ($h = 0$):**
>    Set the quadratic equation to zero:
>    $$-4.90t^2 + 0.96t + 449.36 = 0$$
> 4. **Apply the Quadratic Formula:**
>    $$t = \frac{-0.96 \pm \sqrt{(0.96)^2 - 4(-4.90)(449.36)}}{2(-4.90)}$$
>    Evaluating the roots gives a valid positive timeline root of $t \approx 9.67\text{ seconds}$.

## Power Functions

> [!info] Definition
> A function of the form:
> $$f(x) = x^a$$
> where $a$ is a constant, is called a **power function**. The behavior of the function depends heavily on the nature of the exponent $a$.

---

### Case I: $a = n$, where $n$ is a positive integer

For $n = 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, \dots$, these are single-term polynomials whose structural behavior depends on whether $n$ is **even** or **odd**:

* **Even Power Rules ($n$ is even):** Functions like $y = x^2, x^4, x^6$ are **even functions**. Their graphs look similar to a parabola, symmetric across the $y$-axis.
* **Odd Power Rules ($n$ is odd):** Functions like $y = x, x^3, x^5$ are **odd functions**, displaying point symmetry through the origin.

![[Pasted image 20260625161900.png|center]]


> [!warning] Growth Behavior Near 0 vs. Infinity
> As the power $n$ increases:
> - The graph becomes **flatter** near the origin ($0$) when $|x| < 1$.
> - The graph becomes **steeper** when $|x| \ge 1$.

![[Pasted image 20260625161947.png|center]]

---

### Case II: $a = 1/n$, where $n$ is a positive integer

When $a = \frac{1}{n}$, the function is a **root function**:
$$f(x) = x^{1/n} = \sqrt[n]{x}$$

* **Square Root Behavior ($n = 2$):** $f(x) = \sqrt{x}$. Its graph is the upper half of the sideways parabola $x = y^2$. 
  - **Domain Constraints:** $D = [0, \infty)$ (negative inputs are undefined over $\mathbb{R}$).
* **Cube Root Behavior ($n = 3$):** $f(x) = \sqrt[3]{x}$.
  - **Domain Constraints:** $D = \mathbb{R}$ (every real number has a real cube root). The graph is symmetric through the origin.
* **Generalization:** Any even root function behaves similarly to $\sqrt{x}$, and any odd root function behaves similarly to $\sqrt[3]{x}$.

![[Pasted image 20260625162022.png|center]]

---

### Case III: $a = -1$ (The Reciprocal Function)

When $a = -1$, the power function forms a rational hyperbolic shape:
$$f(x) = x^{-1} = \frac{1}{x}$$

* **Geometric Properties:** The graph is a hyperbola with the coordinate axes acting as its vertical and horizontal asymptotes ($xy = 1$).

## Rational Functions

> [!info] Definition
> A **rational function** $f$ is defined as the ratio of two polynomials:
> $$f(x) = \frac{P(x)}{Q(x)}$$
> where $P(x)$ and $Q(x)$ are polynomials. 
> - **Domain Constraints:** The domain consists of all real values of $x$ such that the denominator does not equal zero:
>   $$\text{Domain} = \{x \in \mathbb{R} \mid Q(x) \neq 0\}$$

### Examples

1. **The Reciprocal Function:** $$f(x) = \frac{1}{x}$$
   - **Domain:** $\{x \mid x \neq 0\}$

2. **Higher-Degree Rational Function:**
   $$f(x) = \frac{2x^4 - x^2 + 1}{x^2 - 4}$$
   - **Domain Analysis:** Set the denominator to zero to find exclusions:
     $$x^2 - 4 = 0 \implies x^2 = 4 \implies x = \pm 2$$
   - **Domain:** $\{x \mid x \neq \pm 2\}$

## Algebraic Functions

> [!info] Definition
> An **algebraic function** is a function that can be constructed using structural algebraic operations starting with polynomials. These operations include:
> - Addition and Subtraction
> - Multiplication and Division
> - Taking roots (fractional exponents)
> 
> *Note: Any rational function is automatically an algebraic function.*

### Examples
* $$f(x) = \sqrt{x^2 + 1}$$
* $$g(x) = \frac{x^4 - 16x^2}{x + \sqrt{x}} + (x - 2)\sqrt[3]{x + 1}$$

![[Pasted image 20260625162710.png|center]]

---

> [!tip] Application: Theory of Relativity
> 
> An important example of an algebraic function occurs in special relativity. The relativistic mass $m$ of a particle moving with velocity $v$ is given by:
> 
> $$m = f(v) = \frac{m_0}{\sqrt{1 - v^2/c^2}}$$
> 
> Where:
> * $m_0$ is the **rest mass** of the particle.
> * $c$ is the **speed of light** in a vacuum ($c \approx 3.0 \times 10^5 \text{ km/s}$).
>

## Trigonometric Functions

> [!warning] Calculus Convention
> In calculus, the absolute convention is that **radian measure is always used** for trigonometric functions unless explicitly indicated otherwise. When evaluating $f(x) = \sin x$, the input $x$ represents an angle measured in radians.

### Sine and Cosine Functions


* **Domain:** $(-\infty, \infty)$
* **Range:** $[-1, 1]$
* **Bounded Property:** $$-1 \le \sin x \le 1 \quad \implies \quad |\sin x| \le 1$$
  $$-1 \le \cos x \le 1 \quad \implies \quad |\cos x| \le 1$$
* **Zeros of Sine:** The roots of $\sin x = 0$ occur at all integer multiples of $\pi$:
  $$x = n\pi \quad \text{where } n \in \mathbb{Z}$$

### Periodicity
Both sine and cosine are periodic functions with a **fundamental period of $2\pi$**. For all real values of $x$:
$$\sin(x + 2\pi) = \sin x \qquad \cos(x + 2\pi) = \cos x$$
![[Pasted image 20260625163637.png|center]]

> [!tip] Applications
> The periodic wave structure of these functions makes them ideal for modeling repeating natural phenomena such as tides, vibrating springs, alternating electric currents, and sound waves.

---

### The Tangent Function

The tangent function is related to sine and cosine by the identity:
$$\tan x = \frac{\sin x}{\cos x}$$


* **Domain Constraints:** It is undefined whenever $\cos x = 0$. This occurs at odd multiples of $\frac{\pi}{2}$:
  $$\text{Domain} = \left\{x \in \mathbb{R} \;\middle|\; x \neq \pm\frac{\pi}{2}, \pm\frac{3\pi}{2}, \dots \right\}$$
* **Range:** $(-\infty, \infty)$
* **Periodicity:** The tangent function has a shifting **period of $\pi$**:
  $$\tan(x + \pi) = \tan x \quad \text{for all } x$$

## Exponential Functions

> [!info] Definition
> **Exponential functions** are functions of the form:
> $$f(x) = a^x$$
> where the base $a$ is a positive constant ($a > 0$).
> - **Domain:** $(-\infty, \infty)$
> - **Range:** $(0, \infty)$

![[Pasted image 20260625163801.png|center]]
### Applications
* **Exponential Growth ($a > 1$):** Used for modeling growth patterns such as population growth or compound interest.
* **Exponential Decay ($a < 1$):** Used for modeling decreasing phenomena such as radioactive decay.

---

## Logarithmic Functions

> [!info] Definition
> **Logarithmic functions** are functions of the form:
> $$f(x) = \log_a x$$
> where the base $a$ is a positive constant. They are the **inverse functions** of the exponential functions.
> - **Domain:** $(0, \infty)$
> - **Range:** $(-\infty, \infty)$
> - **Growth Rate:** The function increases very slowly when $x > 1$.

> [!example] Example 5: Classifying Functions
> Classify the following functions based on their structural mathematical type:
> 
> **(a)** $f(x) = 5^x$
> - **Classification:** **Exponential function**. The independent variable $x$ is located in the exponent.
> 
> **(b)** $g(x) = x^5$
> - **Classification:** **Power function**. The variable $x$ is the base, and it is raised to a constant power. (It can also be classified as a polynomial of degree 5).
> 
> **(c)** $h(x) = \frac{1 + x}{1 - \sqrt{x}}$
> - **Classification:** **Algebraic function**. It is constructed using standard algebraic operations (addition, division, and taking roots) starting with polynomials.
> 
> **(d)** $u(t) = 1 - t + 5t^4$
> - **Classification:** **Polynomial function of degree 4**. It consists of coefficients multiplying non-negative integer powers of the variable $t$.
