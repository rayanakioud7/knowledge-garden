## The Tangent and Velocity Problems

### The Tangent Problem
The word *tangent* is derived from the Latin word *tangens*, which means "touching." 
* **Classic Definition (Euclid):** For a circle, a tangent line is a line that intersects the curve exactly once.
* **General Curve Problem:** For complex curves, a line can touch a point once but not be a tangent, or it can be a tangent line at one point while intersecting the curve multiple times elsewhere.

> [!example] Example 1: Finding a Tangent Line via Limits of Secant Slopes
> Find an equation of the tangent line to the parabola $y = x^2$ at the point $P(1, 1)$.
> 
> **Step-by-Step Mathematical Analysis:**
> 1. **Problem Statement:** To write the line equation, we need its slope $m$, but we only know a single point $P(1,1)$.
> 2. **Secant Line Approximation:** Choose a nearby point $Q(x, x^2)$ on the parabola where $x \neq 1$. The slope of the **secant line** $PQ$ is:
>    $$m_{PQ} = \frac{x^2 - 1}{x - 1}$$
> 3. **Numerical Estimation:** Evaluate $m_{PQ}$ as $Q$ moves closer to $P$:
>    - For $Q(1.5, 2.25) \implies m_{PQ} = \frac{2.25 - 1}{1.5 - 1} = \frac{1.25}{0.5} = 2.5$
>    - For $Q(1.01, 1.0201) \implies m_{PQ} = \frac{1.0201 - 1}{1.01 - 1} = 2.01$
> 
> | $x \to 1^+$ | $m_{PQ}$ | | $x \to 1^-$ | $m_{PQ}$ |
> | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
> | 2 | 3 | | 0 | 1 |
> | 1.5 | 2.5 | | 0.5 | 1.5 |
> | 1.1 | 2.1 | | 0.9 | 1.9 |
> | 1.01 | 2.01 | | 0.99 | 1.99 |
> | 1.001 | 2.001 | | 0.999 | 1.999 |
> 
> 4. **Limiting Form:** As $x$ approaches $1$, the slope $m_{PQ}$ approaches $2$. The slope of the tangent line $t$ is the **limit** of the slopes of the secant lines:
>    $$\lim_{Q \to P} m_{PQ} = m \implies \lim_{x \to 1} \frac{x^2 - 1}{x - 1} = 2$$
> 5. **Line Equation:** Use point-slope form with $m = 2$ at $(1,1)$:
>    $$y - 1 = 2(x - 1) \implies y = 2x - 1$$

## The Velocity Problem

The velocity of an object in real-world environments is rarely constant. While a speedometer reveals a reading at a specific moment, defining this **instantaneous velocity** mathematically requires an analysis of limiting behavior over shrinking time intervals.

---

> [!example] Example 3: Free-Fall Velocity Analysis
> A ball is dropped from the upper observation deck of the CN Tower, $450\text{ m}$ above the ground. Find the instantaneous velocity of the ball after exactly $5\text{ seconds}$.
> 
> **Step-by-Step Solution:**
> 1. **Identify the Position Equation:** Neglecting air resistance, Galileo's law states that the distance fallen $s(t)$ in meters after $t$ seconds is:
>    $$s(t) = 4.9t^2$$
> 2. **The Instantaneous Paradox:** At exactly $t = 5$, there is no elapsed time interval ($\Delta t = 0$), making direct calculation ($\frac{\text{distance}}{\text{time}} = \frac{0}{0}$) impossible.
> 3. **Average Velocity Approximations:** Approximate the value over a brief interval $[5, 5.1]$:
>    $$\text{Average Velocity} = \frac{\text{Change in Position}}{\text{Time Elapsed}} = \frac{s(5.1) - s(5)}{0.1}$$
>    $$\text{Average Velocity} = \frac{4.9(5.1)^2 - 4.9(5)^2}{0.1} = 49.49\text{ m/s}$$
> 4. **Numerical Refinement:** Evaluate average velocities over successively smaller time intervals $[5, 5 + h]$:
> 
> | Time Interval | Average Velocity ($\text{m/s}$) |
> | :--- | :--- |
> | $5 \le t \le 6$ | $53.9$ |
> | $5 \le t \le 5.1$ | $49.49$ |
> | $5 \le t \le 5.05$ | $49.245$ |
> | $5 \le t \le 5.01$ | $49.049$ |
> | $5 \le t \le 5.001$ | $49.0049$ |
> 
> 5. **Limiting Form:** As the time interval shrinks toward 0, the average velocity converges to a stable limit. The **instantaneous velocity** at $t = 5\text{ s}$ is defined as:
>    $$v = 49\text{ m/s}$$

---


> To solve both tangent and velocity problems, one must be able to calculate **limits**.
