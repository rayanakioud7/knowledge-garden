## Transformations of Functions

By applying transformations to the graph of a known function, we can quickly sketch related functions and derive their equations.

### 1. Translations (Shifting)

Let $c > 0$. Vertical shifts change the output value directly ($y$-coordinates), while horizontal shifts alter the input value inside the function argument ($x$-coordinates).

> [!info] Shift Formulas
| Transformation | Algebraic Formula | Geometric Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Vertical Up** | $y = f(x) + c$ | Shift the graph of $f(x)$ upward a distance $c$ |
| **Vertical Down** | $y = f(x) - c$ | Shift the graph of $f(x)$ downward a distance $c$ |
| **Horizontal Right** | $y = f(x - c)$ | Shift the graph of $f(x)$ to the right a distance $c$ |
| **Horizontal Left** | $y = f(x + c)$ | Shift the graph of $f(x)$ to the left a distance $c$ |

### 2. Stretching, Shrinking, and Reflecting

Let $c > 1$. Multiplying by a constant scales the graph coordinates, while changing signs creates mirror reflections.

> [!info] Scaling & Reflection Formulas
| Transformation | Algebraic Formula | Geometric Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Vertical Stretch** | $y = c f(x)$ | Stretch the graph of $f(x)$ vertically by a factor of $c$ |
| **Vertical Shrink** | $y = \frac{1}{c} f(x)$ | Shrink the graph of $f(x)$ vertically by a factor of $c$ |
| **Horizontal Shrink** | $y = f(c x)$ | Compress the graph of $f(x)$ horizontally by a factor of $c$ |
| **Horizontal Stretch** | $y = f(x / c)$ | Expand the graph of $f(x)$ horizontally by a factor of $c$ |
| **Reflection ($x$-axis)** | $y = -f(x)$ | Reflect the graph of $f(x)$ across the $x$-axis |
| **Reflection ($y$-axis)** | $y = f(-x)$ | Reflect the graph of $f(x)$ across the $y$-axis |

> [!example] Example 1: Transformations on Root Functions
> Given the base graph $y = \sqrt{x}$, apply standard transformations:
> - **(b) $y = \sqrt{x} - 2$:** Shift $2$ units downward.
> - **(c) $y = \sqrt{x - 2}$:** Shift $2$ units to the right.
> - **(d) $y = -\sqrt{x}$:** Reflect across the $x$-axis.
> - **(e) $y = 2\sqrt{x}$:** Stretch vertically by a factor of $2$.
> - **(f) $y = \sqrt{-x}$:** Reflect across the $y$-axis.

> [!example] Example 2: Completing the Square for Parabolic Shifts
> Sketch the graph of $f(x) = x^2 + 6x + 10$.
> 
> **Step-by-Step Solution:**
> 1. **Complete the square:**
>    $$y = (x^2 + 6x + 9) - 9 + 10 = (x + 3)^2 + 1$$
> 2. **Identify the base function:** $y = x^2$
> 3. **Apply tracking transformations:**
>    - Horizontal shift: replace $x$ with $x + 3 \implies$ Shift **left** by $3$ units.
>    - Vertical shift: add $1 \implies$ Shift **upward** by $1$ unit.
>    - The vertex moves from $(0,0)$ to $(-3, 1)$.

> [!example] Example 3: Trigonometric Compressions and Reversals
> Sketch the graphs of the following periodic functions:
> 
> **(a) $y = \sin 2x$**
> - **Analysis:** The tracking input modification is $f(2x)$. This compresses the base curve horizontally by a factor of $2$.
> - **Period Impact:** The fundamental period changes from $2\pi$ to $\frac{2\pi}{2} = \pi$.
> 
> ![[Pasted image 20260625172303.png|center]]
> 
> **(b) $y = 1 - \sin x$**
> - **Analysis:** Rearrange to $y = -\sin x + 1$. 
> - **Step 1:** Reflect the baseline $y = \sin x$ across the horizontal $x$-axis to yield $y = -\sin x$.
> - **Step 2:** Shift the inverted profile upward by $1$ unit.
