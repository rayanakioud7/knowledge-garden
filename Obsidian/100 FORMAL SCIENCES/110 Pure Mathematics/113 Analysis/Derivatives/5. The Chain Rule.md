## The Chain Rule

**Motivation:** Functions like $F(x) = \sqrt{x^2 + 1}$ cannot be differentiated using the previous rules alone. Writing $y = f(u) = \sqrt{u}$ with $u = g(x) = x^2 + 1$ gives $F = f \circ g$; we need a rule for composite functions.

**Intuition (rates of change):** If $u$ changes twice as fast as $x$ and $y$ changes three times as fast as $u$, then $y$ changes six times as fast as $x$, i.e.,

$$
\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{dy}{du}\,\frac{du}{dx}
$$

---

### 1. The Chain Rule

> [!info] The Chain Rule
> If $g$ is differentiable at $x$ and $f$ is differentiable at $g(x)$, then the composite $F = f \circ g$ defined by $F(x) = f(g(x))$ is differentiable at $x$ and
>
> $$F'(x) = f'(g(x)) \cdot g'(x) \tag{2}$$
>
> **Leibniz notation:** If $y = f(u)$ and $u = g(x)$ are both differentiable, then
>
> $$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{dy}{du}\,\frac{du}{dx} \tag{3}$$

---

### 3. Outside-Inside Interpretation

$$
\frac{d}{dx}\underbrace{f}_{\text{outer}}(\underbrace{g(x)}_{\text{inner}}) = \underbrace{f'}_{\text{deriv. of outer}}(\underbrace{g(x)}_{\text{eval. at inner}}) \cdot \underbrace{g'(x)}_{\text{deriv. of inner}}
$$

Differentiate the **outer** function (evaluated at the inner), then **multiply** by the derivative of the inner.

---

> [!example] Example 1: $F(x) = \sqrt{x^2 + 1}$
> **Solution 1 (prime notation):** With $f(u) = \sqrt{u}$, $g(x) = x^2 + 1$:
>
> $$f'(u) = \tfrac{1}{2}u^{-1/2} = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{u}}, \qquad g'(x) = 2x$$
>
> $$F'(x) = f'(g(x)) \cdot g'(x) = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{x^2 + 1}} \cdot 2x = \frac{x}{\sqrt{x^2 + 1}}$$
>
> **Solution 2 (Leibniz):** With $u = x^2 + 1$, $y = \sqrt{u}$:
>
> $$F'(x) = \frac{dy}{du}\,\frac{du}{dx} = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{u}}\,(2x) = \frac{x}{\sqrt{x^2 + 1}}$$

> [!warning] Meaning of $dy/dx$ vs $dy/du$
> - $dy/dx = F'(x)$ — derivative of $y$ as a function of $x$.
> - $dy/du = f'(u)$ — derivative of $y$ as a function of $u$.

---

> [!example] Example 2: $\sin(x^2)$ vs $\sin^2 x$
> **(a)** $y = \sin(x^2)$: outer = sine, inner = squaring.
>
> $$\frac{dy}{dx} = \cos(x^2) \cdot 2x = 2x\cos(x^2)$$
>
> **(b)** $y = \sin^2 x = (\sin x)^2$: outer = squaring, inner = sine.
>
> $$\frac{dy}{dx} = 2(\sin x) \cdot \cos x = 2\sin x \cos x = \sin 2x$$

---

### 4. Chain Rule Applied to Trig Functions

If $y = \sin u$ where $u = g(x)$, then

$$
\frac{d}{dx}(\sin u) = \cos u \cdot \frac{du}{dx}
$$

Analogous formulas hold for all six trig functions.

---

### 5. Power Rule Combined with Chain Rule

If $y = [g(x)]^n = u^n$ with $u = g(x)$, then

$$
\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{dy}{du}\,\frac{du}{dx} = nu^{n-1}\,\frac{du}{dx} = n[g(x)]^{n-1}g'(x)
$$

> [!info] The Power Rule + Chain Rule (Formula 4)
> If $n$ is any real number and $u = g(x)$ is differentiable, then
>
> $$\frac{d}{dx}(u^n) = nu^{n-1}\,\frac{du}{dx}$$
>
> Alternatively:
>
> $$\frac{d}{dx}[g(x)]^n = n[g(x)]^{n-1} \cdot g'(x)$$

---

> [!example] Example 3: $y = (x^3 - 1)^{100}$
> Take $u = x^3 - 1$, $n = 100$:
>
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> \frac{dy}{dx} &= 100(x^3 - 1)^{99} \cdot \frac{d}{dx}(x^3 - 1) \\
> &= 100(x^3 - 1)^{99} \cdot 3x^2 = 300x^2(x^3 - 1)^{99}
> \end{aligned}
> $$

---

> [!example] Example 4: $f(x) = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt[3]{x^2 + x + 1}}$
> Rewrite: $f(x) = (x^2 + x + 1)^{-1/3}$.
>
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> f'(x) &= -\tfrac{1}{3}(x^2 + x + 1)^{-4/3}\,\frac{d}{dx}(x^2 + x + 1) \\
> &= -\tfrac{1}{3}(x^2 + x + 1)^{-4/3}(2x + 1)
> \end{aligned}
> $$

---

> [!example] Example 5: $g(t) = \left(\dfrac{t - 2}{2t + 1}\right)^9$
> Combine Power Rule, Chain Rule, and Quotient Rule:
>
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> g'(t) &= 9\left(\frac{t - 2}{2t + 1}\right)^8 \cdot \frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{t - 2}{2t + 1}\right) \\
> &= 9\left(\frac{t - 2}{2t + 1}\right)^8 \cdot \frac{(2t + 1)(1) - (t - 2)(2)}{(2t + 1)^2} \\
> &= \frac{45(t - 2)^8}{(2t + 1)^{10}}
> \end{aligned}
> $$

---

> [!example] Example 6: $y = (2x + 1)^5(x^3 - x + 1)^4$
> Product Rule first, then Chain Rule on each factor:
>
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> \frac{dy}{dx} &= (2x + 1)^5\,\frac{d}{dx}(x^3 - x + 1)^4 + (x^3 - x + 1)^4\,\frac{d}{dx}(2x + 1)^5 \\
> &= (2x + 1)^5 \cdot 4(x^3 - x + 1)^3(3x^2 - 1) + (x^3 - x + 1)^4 \cdot 5(2x + 1)^4 \cdot 2
> \end{aligned}
> $$
>
> Factor out $2(2x + 1)^4(x^3 - x + 1)^3$:
>
> $$\frac{dy}{dx} = 2(2x + 1)^4(x^3 - x + 1)^3(17x^3 + 6x^2 - 9x + 3)$$

---

### 6. Longer Chains

If $y = f(u)$, $u = g(x)$, $x = h(t)$, apply the Chain Rule twice:

$$
\frac{dy}{dt} = \frac{dy}{dx}\,\frac{dx}{dt} = \frac{dy}{du}\,\frac{du}{dx}\,\frac{dx}{dt}
$$

---

> [!example] Example 7: $f(x) = \sin(\cos(\tan x))$
> Chain Rule applied **twice** (peeling from outside):
>
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> f'(x) &= \cos(\cos(\tan x)) \cdot \frac{d}{dx}\cos(\tan x) \\
> &= \cos(\cos(\tan x)) \cdot [-\sin(\tan x)] \cdot \frac{d}{dx}\tan x \\
> &= -\cos(\cos(\tan x))\sin(\tan x)\sec^2 x
> \end{aligned}
> $$

---

> [!example] Example 8: $y = \sqrt{\sec x^3}$
> Layers: outer = square root, middle = secant, inner = cubing.
>
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> \frac{dy}{dx} &= \frac{1}{2\sqrt{\sec x^3}}\,\frac{d}{dx}(\sec x^3) \\
> &= \frac{1}{2\sqrt{\sec x^3}}\,\sec x^3 \tan x^3 \cdot \frac{d}{dx}(x^3) \\
> &= \frac{3x^2 \sec x^3 \tan x^3}{2\sqrt{\sec x^3}}
> \end{aligned}
> $$
