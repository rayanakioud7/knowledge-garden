## Implicit Differentiation

### 1. Explicit vs. Implicit Functions

- **Explicit:** $y$ is given directly in terms of $x$, e.g., $y = \sqrt{x^3 + 1}$ or $y = x\sin x$.
- **Implicit:** $x$ and $y$ are related by an equation, e.g.,

$$
x^2 + y^2 = 25 \tag{1}
$$

$$
x^3 + y^3 = 6xy \tag{2}
$$

**Example (1):** Solving gives $y = \pm\sqrt{25 - x^2}$, defining two functions (upper/lower semicircles).

**Example (2):** The **folium of Descartes**. It implicitly defines $y$ as several functions of $x$, all satisfying $x^3 + [f(x)]^3 = 6x\,f(x)$.

### 2. Method of Implicit Differentiation

1. Differentiate both sides of the equation with respect to $x$, treating $y$ as a function of $x$ (apply Chain Rule to terms in $y$).
2. Solve the resulting equation for $y' = dy/dx$.

---

> [!example] Example 1: Circle $x^2 + y^2 = 25$
> **(a) Find $dy/dx$. 
> (b) Find the tangent at $(3, 4)$.**
>
> **Solution (a):** Differentiate both sides with respect to $x$:
>
> $$
> \frac{d}{dx}(x^2) + \frac{d}{dx}(y^2) = 0
> $$
>
> Since $y$ is a function of $x$, by the Chain Rule:
>
> $$
> \frac{d}{dx}(y^2) = \frac{d}{dy}(y^2)\,\frac{dy}{dx} = 2y\,\frac{dy}{dx}
> $$
>
> Therefore:
>
> $$
> 2x + 2y\,\frac{dy}{dx} = 0 \implies \frac{dy}{dx} = -\frac{x}{y}
> $$
>
> **Solution (b):** At $(3, 4)$: $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = -\dfrac{3}{4}$. Tangent line:
>
> $$
> y - 4 = -\tfrac{3}{4}(x - 3) \quad \text{or} \quad 3x + 4y = 25
> $$

---

> [!example] Example 2: Folium of Descartes $x^3 + y^3 = 6xy$
> **(a) Find $y'$. 
> (b) Tangent at $(3, 3)$. 
> (c) Point of horizontal tangent in Q1.**
>
> **Solution (a):** Differentiate implicitly (Chain Rule on $y^3$, Product Rule on $6xy$):
>
> $$
> 3x^2 + 3y^2 y' = 6xy' + 6y
> $$
>
> Simplify and solve for $y'$:
>
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> x^2 + y^2 y' &= 2xy' + 2y \\
> y^2 y' - 2xy' &= 2y - x^2 \\
> (y^2 - 2x)y' &= 2y - x^2 \\
> y' &= \frac{2y - x^2}{y^2 - 2x}
> \end{aligned}
> $$
>
> **Solution (b):** At $(3, 3)$:
>
> $$y' = \frac{2 \cdot 3 - 3^2}{3^2 - 2 \cdot 3} = \frac{-3}{3} = -1$$
>
> Tangent: $y - 3 = -1(x - 3)$, i.e., $x + y = 6$.
>
> **Solution (c):** Horizontal tangent when $y' = 0$, i.e., $2y - x^2 = 0$ (with $y^2 - 2x \neq 0$). Set $y = \tfrac{1}{2}x^2$ and substitute:
>
> $$x^3 + \left(\tfrac{1}{2}x^2\right)^3 = 6x\left(\tfrac{1}{2}x^2\right)$$
>
> Simplifies to $x^6 = 16x^3$. Since $x \neq 0$ in Q1: $x^3 = 16$, so $x = 2^{4/3}$ and
>
> $$y = \tfrac{1}{2}(2^{8/3}) = 2^{5/3}$$
>
> Horizontal tangent at $(2^{4/3}, 2^{5/3}) \approx (2.5198, 3.1748)$.

> [!info] Note: Advantage of Implicit Differentiation
> Solving $x^3 + y^3 = 6xy$ for $y$ explicitly gives three cubic-formula expressions that are extremely complicated. Some equations, like $y^5 + 3x^2y^2 + 5x^4 = 12$, **cannot** be solved for $y$ at all — yet implicit differentiation still works.

---

> [!example] Example 3: $\sin(x + y) = y^2 \cos x$
> Differentiate implicitly (Chain Rule on LHS; Product + Chain Rule on RHS):
>
> $$
> \cos(x + y) \cdot (1 + y') = y^2(-\sin x) + (\cos x)(2yy')
> $$
>
> Collect $y'$ terms:
>
> $$
> \cos(x + y) + y^2\sin x = (2y\cos x)y' - \cos(x + y)\,y'
> $$
>
> $$
> y' = \frac{y^2\sin x + \cos(x + y)}{2y\cos x - \cos(x + y)}
> $$
>
> **Check:** at $(0, 0)$: $y' = -1$, consistent with the graph.

### 3. Implicit Second Derivative

> [!example] Example 4: Find $y''$ if $x^4 + y^4 = 16$
> **Step 1:** Differentiate implicitly.
>
> $$
> 4x^3 + 4y^3 y' = 0 \implies y' = -\frac{x^3}{y^3} \tag{3}
> $$
>
> **Step 2:** Differentiate $y'$ using the Quotient Rule (remember $y$ is a function of $x$):
>
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> y'' &= \frac{d}{dx}\left(-\frac{x^3}{y^3}\right) = -\frac{y^3\,\frac{d}{dx}(x^3) - x^3\,\frac{d}{dx}(y^3)}{(y^3)^2} \\
> &= -\frac{y^3 \cdot 3x^2 - x^3(3y^2 y')}{y^6}
> \end{aligned}
> $$
>
> **Step 3:** Substitute $y' = -x^3/y^3$ from (3):
>
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> y'' &= -\frac{3x^2 y^3 - 3x^3 y^2\left(-\dfrac{x^3}{y^3}\right)}{y^6} \\
> &= -\frac{3(x^2 y^4 + x^6)}{y^7} = -\frac{3x^2(y^4 + x^4)}{y^7}
> \end{aligned}
> $$
>
> **Step 4:** Use the original relation $x^4 + y^4 = 16$:
>
> $$
> y'' = -\frac{3x^2(16)}{y^7} = -48\,\frac{x^2}{y^7}
> $$
