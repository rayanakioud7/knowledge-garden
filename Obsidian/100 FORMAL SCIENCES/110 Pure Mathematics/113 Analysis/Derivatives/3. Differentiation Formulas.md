## Differentiation Formulas

### Table of Differentiation Formulas

> [!info] Summary of Basic Differentiation Rules
>
> | Rule | Formula |
> |---|---|
> | Constant | $\dfrac{d}{dx}(c) = 0$ |
> | Power | $\dfrac{d}{dx}(x^n) = nx^{n-1}$ |
> | Constant Multiple | $(cf)' = cf'$ |
> | Sum | $(f + g)' = f' + g'$ |
> | Difference | $(f - g)' = f' - g'$ |
> | Product | $(fg)' = fg' + gf'$ |
> | Quotient | $\left(\dfrac{f}{g}\right)' = \dfrac{gf' - fg'}{g^2}$ |

---

### 1. Derivative of a Constant Function

The graph of $f(x) = c$ is a horizontal line with slope $0$.

**Proof from definition:**

$$
f'(x) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h} = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{c - c}{h} = \lim_{h \to 0} 0 = 0
$$

> [!info] Derivative of a Constant Function
> $$\frac{d}{dx}(c) = 0$$

### 2. Power Functions

For $f(x) = x^n$ where $n$ is a positive integer.

**Case $n = 1$:** The graph of $f(x) = x$ is a line with slope $1$.

> [!NOTE]
> $$\frac{d}{dx}(x) = 1 \tag{1}$$

![[Pasted image 20260704170717.png|center|356]]

**Previously established cases ($n = 2, 3$):**

$$
\frac{d}{dx}(x^2) = 2x \qquad \frac{d}{dx}(x^3) = 3x^2 \tag{2}
$$

**Case $n = 4$:** Compute from the definition:

$$
\begin{aligned}
f'(x) &= \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{(x+h)^4 - x^4}{h} \\
&= \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{x^4 + 4x^3h + 6x^2h^2 + 4xh^3 + h^4 - x^4}{h} \\
&= \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{4x^3h + 6x^2h^2 + 4xh^3 + h^4}{h} \\
&= \lim_{h \to 0} (4x^3 + 6x^2h + 4xh^2 + h^3) = 4x^3
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\frac{d}{dx}(x^4) = 4x^3 \tag{3}
$$

**Pattern from (1), (2), (3):** $\dfrac{d}{dx}(x^n) = nx^{n-1}$.

---

### 3. The Power Rule

> [!info] The Power Rule
> If $n$ is a positive integer, then
> $$\frac{d}{dx}(x^n) = nx^{n-1}$$

---

#### First Proof (Factoring)

Using the algebraic identity:

$$
x^n - a^n = (x - a)(x^{n-1} + x^{n-2}a + \cdots + xa^{n-2} + a^{n-1})
$$

Apply the definition:

$$
\begin{aligned}
f'(a) &= \lim_{x \to a} \frac{f(x) - f(a)}{x - a} = \lim_{x \to a} \frac{x^n - a^n}{x - a} \\
&= \lim_{x \to a} (x^{n-1} + x^{n-2}a + \cdots + xa^{n-2} + a^{n-1}) \\
&= a^{n-1} + a^{n-2}a + \cdots + a \cdot a^{n-2} + a^{n-1} \\
&= na^{n-1}
\end{aligned}
$$

---

#### Second Proof (Binomial Theorem)

$$
\begin{aligned}
f'(x) &= \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{(x+h)^n - x^n}{h} \\
&= \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{\left[x^n + nx^{n-1}h + \dfrac{n(n-1)}{2}x^{n-2}h^2 + \cdots + nxh^{n-1} + h^n\right] - x^n}{h} \\
&= \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{nx^{n-1}h + \dfrac{n(n-1)}{2}x^{n-2}h^2 + \cdots + nxh^{n-1} + h^n}{h} \\
&= \lim_{h \to 0} \left[ nx^{n-1} + \frac{n(n-1)}{2}x^{n-2}h + \cdots + nxh^{n-2} + h^{n-1} \right] \\
&= nx^{n-1}
\end{aligned}
$$

Every term except the first contains $h$ as a factor and vanishes. $\blacksquare$

---

> [!example] Example 1: Power Rule Applications
> - **(a)** $f(x) = x^6 \implies f'(x) = 6x^5$
> - **(b)** $y = x^{1000} \implies y' = 1000x^{999}$
> - **(c)** $y = t^4 \implies \dfrac{dy}{dt} = 4t^3$
> - **(d)** $\dfrac{d}{dr}(r^3) = 3r^2$

## New Derivatives from Old

Rules for combining derivatives when functions are formed by addition, subtraction, or multiplication by a constant.

---

### 1. The Constant Multiple Rule

> [!info] The Constant Multiple Rule
> If $c$ is a constant and $f$ is a differentiable function, then
> $$\frac{d}{dx}[cf(x)] = c\,\frac{d}{dx}f(x)$$

**Proof:** Let $g(x) = cf(x)$. Then

$$
\begin{aligned}
g'(x) &= \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{g(x+h) - g(x)}{h} = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{cf(x+h) - cf(x)}{h} \\
&= \lim_{h \to 0} c\left[\frac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h}\right] \\
&= c \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h} \quad \text{(by Law 3 of limits)}\\
&= cf'(x)
\end{aligned}
$$

![[Pasted image 20260704172402.png|center]]

---

> [!example] Example 2
> **(a)** $\dfrac{d}{dx}(3x^4) = 3 \dfrac{d}{dx}(x^4) = 3(4x^3) = 12x^3$
>
> **(b)** $\dfrac{d}{dx}(-x) = \dfrac{d}{dx}[(-1)x] = (-1)\dfrac{d}{dx}(x) = -1$

---

### 2. The Sum Rule

> [!info] The Sum Rule
> If $f$ and $g$ are both differentiable, then
> $$\frac{d}{dx}[f(x) + g(x)] = \frac{d}{dx}f(x) + \frac{d}{dx}g(x)$$
>
> In prime notation: $(f + g)' = f' + g'$.

**Proof:** Let $F(x) = f(x) + g(x)$. Then

$$
\begin{aligned}
F'(x) &= \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{F(x+h) - F(x)}{h} \\
&= \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{[f(x+h) + g(x+h)] - [f(x) + g(x)]}{h} \\
&= \lim_{h \to 0} \left[ \frac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h} + \frac{g(x+h) - g(x)}{h} \right] \\
&= \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h} + \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{g(x+h) - g(x)}{h} \quad \text{(by Law 1)} \\
&= f'(x) + g'(x)
\end{aligned}
$$

**Extension:** Applying twice gives

$$
(f + g + h)' = [(f + g) + h]' = (f+g)' + h' = f' + g' + h'
$$

---

### 3. The Difference Rule

Writing $f - g = f + (-1)g$ and combining the Sum Rule with the Constant Multiple Rule:

> [!info] The Difference Rule
> If $f$ and $g$ are both differentiable, then
> $$\frac{d}{dx}[f(x) - g(x)] = \frac{d}{dx}f(x) - \frac{d}{dx}g(x)$$

---

> [!example] Example 3: Differentiating a Polynomial
> 
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> &\frac{d}{dx}(x^8 + 12x^5 - 4x^4 + 10x^3 - 6x + 5) \\
> &\quad= \frac{d}{dx}(x^8) + 12\frac{d}{dx}(x^5) - 4\frac{d}{dx}(x^4) + 10\frac{d}{dx}(x^3) - 6\frac{d}{dx}(x) + \frac{d}{dx}(5) \\
> &\quad= 8x^7 + 12(5x^4) - 4(4x^3) + 10(3x^2) - 6(1) + 0 \\
> &\quad= 8x^7 + 60x^4 - 16x^3 + 30x^2 - 6
> \end{aligned}
> $$
>

### Example 4: Horizontal Tangents

**Problem:** Find the points on the curve $y = x^4 - 6x^2 + 4$ where the tangent line is horizontal.

**Solution:** Horizontal tangents occur where $dy/dx = 0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{dy}{dx} &= \frac{d}{dx}(x^4) - 6\frac{d}{dx}(x^2) + \frac{d}{dx}(4) \\
&= 4x^3 - 12x + 0 = 4x(x^2 - 3)
\end{aligned}
$$

Set $dy/dx = 0$:

$$
4x(x^2 - 3) = 0 \implies x = 0 \quad \text{or} \quad x = \pm\sqrt{3}
$$

**Corresponding points:**

| $x$ | $y = x^4 - 6x^2 + 4$ |
|---|---|
| $0$ | $4$ |
| $\sqrt{3}$ | $-5$ |
| $-\sqrt{3}$ | $-5$ |

Horizontal tangents at $(0, 4)$, $(\sqrt{3}, -5)$, and $(-\sqrt{3}, -5)$.

## The Product Rule

> [!warning] Common Mistake
> The derivative of a product is **not** the product of the derivatives.
>
> **Counterexample:** Let $f(x) = x$ and $g(x) = x^2$. Then $f'(x) = 1$, $g'(x) = 2x$, so $f'g' = 2x$. But $(fg)(x) = x^3$ gives $(fg)'(x) = 3x^2$. Thus $(fg)' \neq f'g'$.

---

### 1. The Product Rule

> [!info] The Product Rule
> If $f$ and $g$ are both differentiable, then
> $$\frac{d}{dx}[f(x)g(x)] = f(x)\frac{d}{dx}[g(x)] + g(x)\frac{d}{dx}[f(x)]$$
>
> In prime notation: $(fg)' = fg' + gf'$.

**In words:** the derivative of a product of two functions is *the first function times the derivative of the second, plus the second function times the derivative of the first*.

---

### Proof

Let $F(x) = f(x)g(x)$. Then

$$
F'(x) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{F(x+h) - F(x)}{h} = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x+h)g(x+h) - f(x)g(x)}{h}
$$

**Trick:** Subtract and add $f(x+h)g(x)$ in the numerator to separate $f$ and $g$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
F'(x) &= \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x+h)g(x+h) - f(x+h)g(x) + f(x+h)g(x) - f(x)g(x)}{h} \\
&= \lim_{h \to 0} \left[ f(x+h)\,\frac{g(x+h) - g(x)}{h} + g(x)\,\frac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h} \right] \\
&= \lim_{h \to 0} f(x+h) \cdot \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{g(x+h) - g(x)}{h} + \lim_{h \to 0} g(x) \cdot \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h} \\
&= f(x)g'(x) + g(x)f'(x)
\end{aligned}
$$

**Justifications used:**
- $\lim_{h \to 0} g(x) = g(x)$ since $g(x)$ is constant with respect to $h$.
- $\lim_{h \to 0} f(x+h) = f(x)$ because $f$ is differentiable at $x$, hence continuous at $x$ (Theorem 2.2.4). $\blacksquare$

---

> [!example] Example 6: Applying the Product Rule
> 
> **Problem:** Find $F'(x)$ if $F(x) = (6x^3)(7x^4)$.
> 
> **Solution:**
> 
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> F'(x) &= (6x^3)\,\frac{d}{dx}(7x^4) + (7x^4)\,\frac{d}{dx}(6x^3) \\
> &= (6x^3)(28x^3) + (7x^4)(18x^2) \\
> &= 168x^6 + 126x^6 = 294x^6
> \end{aligned}
> $$
> 
> **Verification** (multiplying first):
> 
> $$
> F(x) = (6x^3)(7x^4) = 42x^7 \implies F'(x) = 42(7x^6) = 294x^6 \checkmark
> $$
> 

> [!info] Note
> For products like $y = x^2 \sin x$, expanding is not possible — the Product Rule is the only method.

---

> [!example] Example 7: Product Rule with an Unknown Function
> 
> **Problem:** If $h(x) = xg(x)$ and $g(3) = 5$, $g'(3) = 2$, find $h'(3)$.
> 
> **Solution:** By the Product Rule,
> 
> $$
> h'(x) = \frac{d}{dx}[xg(x)] = x\,\frac{d}{dx}[g(x)] + g(x)\,\frac{d}{dx}[x] = xg'(x) + g(x)
> $$
> 
> Therefore:
> 
> $$
> h'(3) = 3g'(3) + g(3) = 3 \cdot 2 + 5 = 11
> $$
>

## The Quotient Rule

> [!info] The Quotient Rule
> If $f$ and $g$ are differentiable, then
> $$\frac{d}{dx}\left[\frac{f(x)}{g(x)}\right] = \frac{g(x)\dfrac{d}{dx}[f(x)] - f(x)\dfrac{d}{dx}[g(x)]}{[g(x)]^2}$$
>
> In prime notation: $\left(\dfrac{f}{g}\right)' = \dfrac{gf' - fg'}{g^2}$.

**In words:** the derivative of a quotient is *the denominator times the derivative of the numerator, minus the numerator times the derivative of the denominator, all divided by the square of the denominator*.

---

### Proof

Let $F(x) = f(x)/g(x)$. Then

$$
F'(x) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{F(x+h) - F(x)}{h} = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{\dfrac{f(x+h)}{g(x+h)} - \dfrac{f(x)}{g(x)}}{h} = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x+h)g(x) - f(x)g(x+h)}{hg(x+h)g(x)}
$$

Subtract and add $f(x)g(x)$ in the numerator to separate $f$ and $g$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
F'(x) &= \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x+h)g(x) - f(x)g(x) + f(x)g(x) - f(x)g(x+h)}{h\,g(x+h)g(x)} \\
&= \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{g(x)\,\dfrac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h} - f(x)\,\dfrac{g(x+h) - g(x)}{h}}{g(x+h)g(x)} \\
&= \frac{\displaystyle\lim_{h \to 0} g(x) \cdot \lim_{h \to 0}\dfrac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h} - \lim_{h \to 0} f(x) \cdot \lim_{h \to 0}\dfrac{g(x+h) - g(x)}{h}}{\displaystyle\lim_{h \to 0} g(x+h) \cdot \lim_{h \to 0} g(x)} \\
&= \frac{g(x)f'(x) - f(x)g'(x)}{[g(x)]^2}
\end{aligned}
$$

**Justification:** $g$ is continuous by Theorem 2.2.4, so $\lim_{h \to 0} g(x+h) = g(x)$. $\blacksquare$

---

### Consequence

Any **polynomial** is differentiable on $\mathbb{R}$, and any **rational function** is differentiable on its domain.

---

> [!example] Example 8: Differentiating a Rational Function
> 
> **Problem:** Let $y = \dfrac{x^2 + x - 2}{x^3 + 6}$. Find $y'$.
> 
> **Solution:**
> 
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> y' &= \frac{(x^3 + 6)\dfrac{d}{dx}(x^2 + x - 2) - (x^2 + x - 2)\dfrac{d}{dx}(x^3 + 6)}{(x^3 + 6)^2} \\
> &= \frac{(x^3 + 6)(2x + 1) - (x^2 + x - 2)(3x^2)}{(x^3 + 6)^2} \\
> &= \frac{(2x^4 + x^3 + 12x + 6) - (3x^4 + 3x^3 - 6x^2)}{(x^3 + 6)^2} \\
> &= \frac{-x^4 - 2x^3 + 6x^2 + 12x + 6}{(x^3 + 6)^2}
> \end{aligned}
> $$
>

>

> Rewrite quotients into simpler forms when possible. For example,
>

> is easier to differentiate after dividing:
>

## General Power Functions

Extending the Power Rule to negative-integer, zero, rational, and real exponents.

---

### 1. Negative Integer Exponents

> [!info] Power Rule (Negative Integers)
> If $n$ is a positive integer, then
> $$\frac{d}{dx}(x^{-n}) = -nx^{-n-1}$$

**Proof:** Apply the Quotient Rule to $x^{-n} = 1/x^n$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{d}{dx}(x^{-n}) = \frac{d}{dx}\left(\frac{1}{x^n}\right) &= \frac{x^n \dfrac{d}{dx}(1) - 1 \cdot \dfrac{d}{dx}(x^n)}{(x^n)^2} \\
&= \frac{x^n \cdot 0 - 1 \cdot nx^{n-1}}{x^{2n}} \\
&= \frac{-nx^{n-1}}{x^{2n}} = -nx^{n-1-2n} = -nx^{-n-1}
\end{aligned}
$$ $\blacksquare$

---

> [!example] Example 9
> **(a)** If $y = \dfrac{1}{x}$, then
> $$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{d}{dx}(x^{-1}) = -x^{-2} = -\frac{1}{x^2}$$
>
> **(b)** $\dfrac{d}{dt}\left(\dfrac{6}{t^3}\right) = 6\,\dfrac{d}{dt}(t^{-3}) = 6(-3)t^{-4} = -\dfrac{18}{t^4}$

---

### 2. Extending to All Real Exponents

- **$n = 0$:** $x^0 = 1$ has derivative $0$, matching $0 \cdot x^{-1} = 0$. ✓
- Combined with positive/negative integer cases: Power Rule holds for **any integer $n$**.
- **Fractional case ($n = 1/2$):** From Example 3 in Section 2.2,
$$\frac{d}{dx}\sqrt{x} = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{x}} \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \frac{d}{dx}(x^{1/2}) = \tfrac{1}{2}x^{-1/2}$$
also matches the pattern.

The rule generalizes to **any real number** $n$ (proved in Chapter 6).

---

> [!info] The Power Rule (General Version)
> If $n$ is any real number, then
> $$\frac{d}{dx}(x^n) = nx^{n-1}$$

---

> [!example] Example 10: General Power Rule
> 
> **(a)** If $f(x) = x^\pi$, then $f'(x) = \pi x^{\pi - 1}$.
> 
> **(b)** Let $y = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt[3]{x^2}} = x^{-2/3}$. Then:
> 
> $$
> \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{d}{dx}(x^{-2/3}) = -\tfrac{2}{3}x^{-(2/3) - 1} = -\tfrac{2}{3}x^{-5/3}
> $$
> 

---

> [!example] Example 11: Product Rule + General Power Rule
> 
> **Problem:** Differentiate $f(t) = \sqrt{t}\,(a + bt)$.
> 
> **Solution 1 (Product Rule):**
> 
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> f'(t) &= \sqrt{t}\,\frac{d}{dt}(a + bt) + (a + bt)\,\frac{d}{dt}(\sqrt{t}) \\
> &= \sqrt{t} \cdot b + (a + bt) \cdot \tfrac{1}{2}t^{-1/2} \\
> &= b\sqrt{t} + \frac{a + bt}{2\sqrt{t}} = \frac{a + 3bt}{2\sqrt{t}}
> \end{aligned}
> $$
>
