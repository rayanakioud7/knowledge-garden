# Mesopotamia

> [!ABSTRACT] The Cradle of Civilization
> 
> **Mesopotamia** (from the Greek $mesos$ "middle" and $potamos$ "river") refers to the "Land Between the Rivers". It is the site of the world's earliest urban civilization, developing in the fertile region between the **Tigris and Euphrates rivers**. This area served as a cultural epicenter whose influence radiated throughout the Middle East, reaching as far as Egypt and the Indus Valley.

---

## I. Geographical Scope

While the name specifically refers to the land between the two rivers, the historical region is broadly defined to include:
- **Most of modern-day Iraq**.
- **Eastern Syria**.
- **Southeastern Turkey**.

---

## II. Historical Timeline

The history of Mesopotamia is a 4,000-year saga of shifting empires and technological leaps, beginning in prehistory and concluding with the Arab conquests of the 7th century CE.

|**Period**|**Key Eras & Cultures**|**Major Contributions**|
|---|---|---|
|**Prehistoric**|Ubaid & Uruk Periods|First cities, invention of the wheel.|
|**Sumerian**|3rd Millennium BCE|Cuneiform writing, sexagesimal math.|
|**Old Babylonian**|c. 18th Century BCE|**Hammurabi’s Code**, "Golden Age" of math.|
|**Assyrian**|c. 900–612 BCE|Military bureaucracy, massive libraries.|
|**Seleucid/Greek**|3rd Century BCE|Advanced Mathematical Astronomy.|

---

## III. The [[Scribes & Knowledge Transmission|Scribal]] Foundation

The preservation of Mesopotamian history is largely due to the use of **clay tablets**. Unlike the fragile papyrus used in **[[Mathematics in ancient Egypt|Ancient Egypt]]**, these durable, fire-hardened tablets allowed for the survival of sophisticated administrative, mathematical, and literary records.

- **Information Managers:** **[[Scribes & Knowledge Transmission]]** were the elite professionals responsible for the administration and preservation of knowledge.
- **Education:** The scribal system was highly structured, moving from basic lists to complex literary compositions.
- **Positional Math:** Mesopotamians developed a **Sexagesimal (Base 60)** system, the legacy of which remains in our modern measurement of time and angles.

---

## IV. Legacy & Influence

Mesopotamian culture laid the groundwork for numerous global advancements:

- **Writing:** The development of **[[Cuneiform]]**.
- **Mathematics:** Early mastery of the **[[Pythagorean Theorem]]** and quadratic logic.
- **Urbanism:** The foundation of the world's first cities and centralized administration.
